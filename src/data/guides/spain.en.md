## Spain in 2026: what actually changed

Three changes over the past eighteen months have reshaped how you get into Spain, and one of them will save you money immediately.

**The Golden Visa is gone.** Organic Law **1/2025** ended the property-investor route on **3 April 2025**. Any agency still advertising Spanish residency in exchange for a EUR 500,000 property purchase is selling something that no longer exists in law. If you take one thing from this guide, take that.

**Regularisation got a year shorter.** The new Immigration Regulation (Royal Decree **1155/2024**, in force since **20 May 2025**) cut the continuous-residence requirement for *arraigo social* and *arraigo sociolaboral* from three years to **two**, restructured arraigo into five distinct routes, and extended initial permits to one year with four-year renewals.

**The nomad threshold went up.** Royal Decree 126/2026 raised the minimum wage 3.1% to **EUR 1,221 per month** across fourteen payments, which pushes the Digital Nomad Visa income requirement to **EUR 2,849 per month**.

One thing did *not* change, and it matters just as much: **naturalisation still takes ten years** of legal residence for nationals of Ghana, Kenya, the UAE and New Zealand. The famous two-year route applies only to citizens of Latin American countries, the Philippines, Equatorial Guinea and Portugal.

## The main legal routes

### 1) Digital Nomad Visa — Visado de Teletrabajo Internacional

The most-searched Spanish route of 2026, created by Law 28/2022, the "Startup Law".

| Requirement | 2026 figure |
| --- | --- |
| Monthly income (main applicant) | **EUR 2,849** (200% of the minimum wage) |
| First family member | **+EUR 916** per month (75% of SMI) |
| Each additional member | **+EUR 305** per month (25% of SMI) |
| Source of income | At least **80%** from outside Spain |
| Employer's age | Trading for at least **one year** |
| Your tenure | At least **three months** with that employer |
| Qualification | A degree, or **three years** of professional experience |
| Health cover | Full coverage valid in Spain |
| Police clearance | From every country you lived in over the last **five years** |

**The strategic point most guides miss:** there are two doors, not one.

- **At a consulate abroad**, you get a **one-year** visa and renew from inside Spain.
- **From inside Spain** as a lawful visitor, through the **UGE-CE** unit, you get a **three-year residence permit** straight away, renewable for two more, decided in roughly **20 working days**, with **positive administrative silence**.

The second door is faster and lasts longer, but it assumes you can enter Spain lawfully first. In practice that suits **New Zealand and UAE nationals**, who are visa-exempt for short stays. Ghanaian and Kenyan applicants normally use the consular route, because entering at all requires a visa issued in advance.

**Tax:** you may elect the special regime — commonly called the Beckham Law — and pay a flat **24%** on income up to EUR 600,000 for six years, instead of a progressive scale reaching 47%.

### 2) Non-Lucrative Visa — Residencia No Lucrativa

For retirees and anyone living on passive income: pensions, rent, investments or savings.

| Requirement | 2026 figure |
| --- | --- |
| Annual income (main applicant) | **EUR 28,800** — EUR 2,400 a month (400% of IPREM) |
| Each additional member | **+EUR 7,200** a year (EUR 600 a month) |
| IPREM 2026 | **EUR 600** a month — frozen since 2023 |
| Health cover | Private, from a Spanish-licensed insurer, **with no co-payment** |
| Validity | One year, then two-year renewals |

**The constraint that catches people out:** this permit **does not allow you to work** — and that includes remote work for a foreign employer. If you work remotely, you need the nomad route. Conflating the two is a common reason for refusal, and a more common reason for a renewal to fail later.

**On the insurance:** the "no co-payment" rule disqualifies most cheap international travel policies. You need a policy from an insurer licensed in Spain with cover equivalent to the public system.

### 3) Arraigo — regularisation after two years

For people already living in Spain. After the 2025 reform:

| Route | Residence required | Core condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arraigo social | **Two years** continuous | A job offer or sufficient means, plus social ties |
| Arraigo sociolaboral | **Two years** | Evidence of a prior employment relationship |
| Arraigo socioformativo | **Two years** | Enrolment in accredited vocational training |
| Arraigo familiar | Case by case | Family ties to a Spanish national |
| Arraigo laboral | Case by case | Evidence of undeclared work |

Decisions are due within **three months**, and here administrative silence is **negative** — no answer means an implied refusal you can appeal, not an approval.

### 4) Employer-sponsored work permit — Cuenta Ajena

The conventional route: a contract with a Spanish employer, subject to a labour-market test unless the occupation appears on the quarterly **Catálogo de Ocupaciones de Difícil Cobertura**, which is dominated by maritime, health and technical roles. Check [in-demand jobs in Spain](/en/jobs?country=es) before negotiating, and measure the offer against the [cost of living in Madrid](/en/explorer?to=es).

### 🇬🇭 Ghana — applying in Accra

- **Application centre:** Spain contracts **BLS International** in some countries and **VFS Global** in others, and these contracts have changed more than once. Confirm the current provider on the Spanish embassy's own site before booking — an appointment with the wrong provider costs you the slot and the fee.
- **Police certificate:** issued by the **Criminal Investigation Department (CID)** at Ghana Police headquarters in Accra, typically two to four weeks. Ask explicitly for the international-use version.
- **Legalisation:** available sources disagree on whether Ghana has joined the Apostille Convention, so **assume the traditional chain**: notary → **Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Regional Integration** → Spanish consular section. Check the HCCH status table first; the difference between the two paths is weeks and fees.
- **Translation:** every non-Spanish document needs a sworn translation (*traductor jurado*) recognised by the Spanish foreign ministry. Ordinary local translations are rejected.

### 🇰🇪 Kenya — applying in Nairobi

- **Police certificate:** the **Certificate of Good Conduct** from the DCI, requested through **eCitizen** with fingerprints taken at a DCI office.
- **Bank statement rules:** the statement is what actually proves your income. In practice: **six consecutive months**, stamped by the branch, with a covering letter from the bank stating the account number, holder and balance. App-generated PDFs without a stamp are routinely refused.
- **Degree authentication:** recognition runs through the **Kenya National Qualifications Authority (KNQA)** and, for university awards, the **Commission for University Education (CUE)**, then legalisation via the **Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Diaspora Affairs**.
- For regulated professions — medicine, nursing, engineering, teaching — Spain additionally requires *homologación* or *equivalencia* from the education ministry, a separate process that can take months. Start it in parallel with the visa, not after.

### 🇦🇪 United Arab Emirates — applying where you live, not where you are from

- **Residence permit validity:** consulates generally require your UAE residence to be valid for **at least three months** (some ask six) beyond your intended departure. A residence permit about to expire usually means the mission declines jurisdiction.
- **No-objection certificate:** a letter from your employer on letterhead stating job title, salary, start date and consent to travel. Self-employed applicants substitute the trade licence and memorandum of association.
- **Bank evidence:** **six months** of stamped statements from a UAE bank plus a salary certificate. Missions look at **deposit regularity** rather than the closing balance; a large lump sum deposited days before filing raises questions instead of settling them.
- **Attestation:** personal documents need **UAE MOFA** attestation followed by the Spanish consulate.
- **UAE nationals** enter Schengen without a short-stay visa, but long-stay residence still requires a **national (D) visa** obtained in advance. The exemption does not substitute for it.

### 🇳🇿 New Zealand — a strong passport, with a hard limit

- **Short stays:** visa-free for **90 days in any 180**.
- **The limit that matters:** visa-free entry **does not convert** into residence. Unlike Germany, Spain requires a **national (D) visa obtained abroad** for most routes. The one practical exception is the **UGE-CE** nomad route, which accepts applications filed from inside Spain during a lawful stay.
- **Apostille:** New Zealand is a Hague member, so documents are legalised with a single **apostille** from the Department of Internal Affairs rather than a consular chain.
- **Working Holiday:** open to applicants aged 18–30 for up to twelve months under an annual quota. Excellent for testing the market, but it does **not always count** toward the residence years that lead to permanent residency — verify before building a long-term plan on it.
- **EES:** the biometric Entry/Exit System has been fully operational at every Schengen external border since **10 April 2026**. Passports are no longer stamped by hand; fingerprints and a facial image are recorded, and the 90-day count is now automatic and exact.
